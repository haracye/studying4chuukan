Key Terms — 重要用語じゅうようようごkey terms
Glossary of terms from this unit
補数ほすう complement (No. 3)
A number that lets a computer perform subtraction using only addition circuits.
基数の補数きすうのほすう radix complement (No. 4)
The smallest number that causes a one-digit carry when added. In decimal, the complement of 6 is 4 (6 + 4 = 10).
減基数の補数げんきすうのほすう diminished radix complement (No. 5)
The largest number that does not cause a carry when added. In decimal, the complement of 6 is 3 (6 + 3 = 9).
減基数げんきすう diminished radix (base − 1)
The base minus one: 9 in decimal, 1 in binary. The base for the diminished radix complement.
2の補数にのほすう two's complement
The radix complement in binary, found by inverting all bits and adding 1 (0011 → 1100 → 1101).
1の補数いちのほすう one's complement
The diminished radix complement in binary: simply invert all bits (0011 → 1100).
符号ふごう sign
The plus/minus mark of a number. In signed binary, the leading bit acts as the sign: 0 = plus, 1 = minus.
反転はんてん inversion (bit flip)
Flipping every bit: 0 becomes 1 and 1 becomes 0. Step ① of finding a complement.
桁上がりけたあがり carry
When a sum exceeds a digit place and moves up to the next one — e.g. 6 + 4 = 10 in decimal.
最上位さいじょうい most significant digit
The leftmost, highest-value digit. In complement subtraction, its overflow is ignored.
溢れあふれ overflow
The part of a result that exceeds the available bits (e.g. the leading 1 of 10100). Ignored in complement arithmetic.
代用だいよう substitution
Using one thing in place of another — computers substitute subtraction with addition using complements.
回路かいろ circuit
The electronic pathways inside a computer. Addition circuits alone can handle both addition and subtraction.
